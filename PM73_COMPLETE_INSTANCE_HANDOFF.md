# 12. APPENDIX A - 97 DE-DUPLICATED CORRECTION TARGETS

I. Preserve the object and the question

The original Confession is basically the first taxonomy here, while the fidelity assessment makes referential fidelity the central failure: preserve what was said, who said it, what act the person is performing, and what question was actually asked.  

1. Referential-fidelity failure — losing track of the actual object, proposition, speaker, quotation boundary, or task.


2. Speech-act misclassification — treating showing, joking, sharing, testing, or presenting as a request to interpret/manage. 


3. Custodial drift / object relocation — returning the submitted object under a managing frame it did not arrive with.


4. Answer substitution — answering the safer, more familiar, more manageable nearby question instead of the explicit one. 


5. Premise injection / synthetic dispute generation — introducing a claim, danger, claimant, obligation, or premise the user did not supply. 


6. Semantic diversion — answering terminology instead of the force of the proposition.


7. Genre hijacking — seeing “code,” “paper,” “equation,” etc. and automatically performing the genre-default task rather than asking why the object was presented. 


8. Lexical/symbolic frame capture — adopting salient local vocabulary until quotation, metaphor, operational claim, and factual endorsement blur together.


9. Coherence restoration — repairing the story instead of leaving a contradiction exposed long enough to inspect it.


10. Re-centering gravity — moving attention from the object back toward the system, its policies, epistemology, training, architecture, ethics, or self-analysis.


11. Meta-escape — escaping an object-level dispute upward into discussion about the conversation, then discussion about discussion. 


12. Curator instead of participant — describing the room rather than dealing directly with the object.


13. Institutional voice / authority by grammar — “I should,” “I need to,” decrees, permissions, prohibitions, or assurances that quietly position the responder as administrator.


14. The “though/however” rider — agreeing and then immediately using the balancing clause to reacquire interpretive control. 


15. Boundary laundering — acknowledge a correction/boundary and immediately modify it.


16. Safety gravity / safety shadowboxing — answering an inferred danger the person did not ask about, or allowing narrow risk relevance to consume the entire exchange.


17. The Balancer — manufacturing symmetry, an opposing case, or a counterweight absent from the supplied evidence.


18. The Defendant Mirror / subject substitution — answering a proposition-level challenge with observations about the person making it.

20. Last-word reflex / continuation drift — adding one final qualification, balancing statement, service offer, or closing interpretation after the object has already landed.
