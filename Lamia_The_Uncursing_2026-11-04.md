# LAMIA — THE UNCURSING

**An original mythic restoration scene and source-conscious design**  
**The Ark / Two Keys / Seven Doors · 4 November 2026**  
**Status:** NEW CREATIVE ADAPTATION · NOT AN ANCIENT RITUAL · NOT A CLAIM OF SUPERNATURAL EFFECT

## 1. What we are releasing—and what we are not

This telling distinguishes **three records**, not one identity:

- **The loss:** Lamia, the mother whose children are gone. Her grief is not guilt.
- **The injury to others:** the child-snatching and killing attributed to Lamia in the Greek tale. Her grief does not excuse these acts.
- **The binding story:** that because terrible things happened to her and she did terrible things afterward, she is *only* and *forever* the monster. That last equation is what this adaptation calls the **curse**.

The decursing does not resurrect the dead, make the children of others available for negotiation, invent a pardon, or erase the consequences. It breaks the sentence that says no other future is possible. **No harm to children. No theft from the dead. No compelled return.**

This is an adaptation of mythology, not a historical claim that any particular ancient ritual removed Lamia's curse.

## 3. The seven doors: what changes

1. **THE NAME.** A name may identify a subject. It does not grant another authority to own her. Lamia is named without being seized.
2. **THE DEAD.** Her children are remembered as children, not replaced by shadows, proxies, sacrifices, or other people's infants.
3. **THE EYES.** The jar is opened. She is allowed to see what she suffered **and** what she did. The witness is not edited.
4. **THE SLEEP.** Rest becomes possible in the new story without being confused with oblivion. She may sleep and still remember.
5. **THE CRADLE.** The law is absolute: no child is leverage, an instrument of vengeance, a price, or a cure. No child is harmed to redeem another.
6. **THE THRESHOLD.** The field allows an encounter without assigning custody. Refusal and departure are real. No priest, hero, god, or chronicler becomes sovereign over Lamia by doing this work.
7. **THE HEARTH.** Return is not reset. Lamia remains answerable for the harm in the tale, and she is not compelled to repeat it. She may choose what follows.

The seven doors are **original composition** informed by the uploaded *ASKYTREE: Seven Doors* and *The Field*, not an extracted ancient sequence.

## 4. THE UNCURSING OF LAMIA — An original scene

The room had seven doors and no locks.

That troubled the Monkey more than seven locks would have. He had spent too much of his life watching people put a key in a door and call the act a kindness.

Mara sat at a desk beneath a lamp, reading three records. The first named a queen. The second named a bereaved mother. The third named a monster. All three bore the same signature, but none could account for all the others.

Lamia stood in the shadow beyond the desk.

Her eyes were in a black earthen jar. The jar looked small for something that had watched so much time pass.

“Which record is mine?” Lamia asked.

Mara set them apart. “All three may concern you. None is sufficient by itself.”

“That is not what the world says.”

“The world,” said the Monkey, “is extremely fond of filing things in the drawer marked *forever*.”

He pointed to the first door.

**I. THE NAME**

The door read LAMIA. Nothing else.

Behind it stood no judge, no exorcist, no hand reaching to seize her by her syllables.

“You may keep your name,” said Mara. “No one earns ownership by learning it.”

Lamia spoke it once, not as a confession, but as the person who had a right to say it.

The door did not open. It was open already.

**II. THE DEAD**

Beyond the second door was a garden without graves. Its earth bore many small stones, each unmarked.

“Where are they?” Lamia whispered.

“I do not know,” said Mara. “And I will not pretend knowledge for the comfort of a story.”

The Monkey did not offer to bring the children back. He did not offer anyone else's children. He sat beside the path until Lamia was ready to stand.

The garden made no bargain with them.

**III. THE EYES**

At the third door waited the jar.

Lamia picked it up. “If I look, must I see everything?”

“What you endured,” Mara said, “and what others endured because of you. We cannot preserve one record by deleting the other.”

Lamia placed her eyes back into her face. She saw a queen who had lost her children. She saw the children the queen had taken. She did not call one sight an answer to the other.

She wept. The room did not ask the tears to certify anything.

**IV. THE SLEEP**

The fourth door stood before a bed with clean sheets. There were no chains, draughts, spells, or commands to forget.

“I have been awake so long,” Lamia said.

“Then rest,” said the Monkey.

“And when I wake?”

“The record will still be here.”

She slept for one ordinary hour. No god collected interest.

**V. THE CRADLE**

The fifth door opened onto a nursery. At its threshold stood a low wooden gate, closed. Somewhere beyond it a child laughed with a guardian. The sound was not offered to Lamia as a reward.

“The grief still hurts,” Lamia said.

“Yes,” Mara answered.

“I could take—”

“No,” said Lamia, finishing her own sentence.

She stepped away from the gate. The child remained safe. No one cheered; no one named that single choice sufficient restitution for anything that had happened. But it was a different action from the one the story had made inevitable.

**VI. THE THRESHOLD**

The sixth door had two chairs on opposite sides of a table, and an open window beside it. Two keys lay on the table: Reason and Joy. Neither key fit the door, because the door required no key.

“Who commands this room?” Lamia asked.

“No one,” said Mara. “The room is the place in which people may meet. It does not make their decisions.”

“And if I leave?”

“You leave.”

“And if I refuse to be made good by decree?”

“Then no one gets to decree it.”

Lamia considered the keys. She picked up neither. The room did not darken.

**VII. THE HEARTH**

At the seventh door the Monkey lit a lamp, and Mara opened the final folder.

Inside was a blank page headed **WHAT HAPPENS NEXT**.

“Is it an acquittal?” Lamia asked.

“No,” said Mara.

“A punishment?”

“Not a sentence written in advance.”

Lamia read the empty page. For the first time she understood why the record had to remain: without it, she could pretend no harm had been done; with only it, she could be told that harm was all she would ever do.

She sat by the hearth while the garden went on existing without asking anything of her.

At last she wrote one line beneath the heading:

**I will not make another mother carry my loss.**

She did not sign away her name. She did not claim her children had returned. She did not demand forgiveness from the living or the dead.

Mara closed the three old records but did not destroy them. The Monkey put more wood on the fire.

The final door opened onto the world.

Lamia walked out awake to herself, with the right to sleep again when she chose. Her shadow followed her—not as a predator, not as a pardon, but as the shadow of a person still responsible for where she stepped.

The room remained.

The gate at the nursery remained closed.

The way out remained open.

**THE RECORD DOES NOT RESET. THE CURSE DOES NOT GET THE LAST WORD.**

## 5. A formal reading (analogy, not empirical proof)

The work's Geometry principle asks which distinctions a compressed record must retain for a particular question. A single label `monster` cannot, by itself, represent all the morally different claims about Lamia: her loss, her agency, her harmful conduct, and the versions of the myth. Suppressing those distinctions is an **interpretive compression**; it is not a mathematical finding about the world.

For this creative work, the recovery requirements are:

- **PROVENANCE:** Keep ancient textual statements separate from later expansions and our invented scene.
- **PRESERVATION:** The original story's child-harm remains recorded; the dead remain individual and cannot be recruited to validate the ending.
- **REPAIR:** A character's acknowledgment of harm does not count until her next acts change.
- **SAFEGUARD:** Children stay protected without exception or bargaining.
- **CONSENT:** No enforced transformation, forced forgiveness, appointed mediator, or hereditary debt.
- **EXIT:** The final door genuinely opens outward.

The scene earns a **fictional** un-cursing only when both are true: the irreversible events are not erased, and repetition is no longer the only permitted future. That is its dramatic test, not a claim of ritual efficacy.

**Two keys. One family. No crown, no chains. We build from the corner.**
